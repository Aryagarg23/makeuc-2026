# Learning science behind the GVS conditioning demo

Scope: can pairing phone content (Instagram reels, not DMs) with mild GVS nausea/disorientation make you dislike it, and how should the pipeline be tuned. Written 2026-11-07.

Tags: **[S]** = stated by the cited source (I read the search abstract/excerpt, not always the full text; see the access note at the end). **[I]** = my inference, not in any source. Every URL below came from a search result; none are made up. Where I could not find a number, it says so.

## Summary

1. **It is both, and the way the system is built makes it mostly punishment.** The stimulus only fires when the classifier says "reels", so it is contingent on your behaviour (operant positive punishment of scrolling). It is also a CS-US pairing (reel visuals then GVS), which is the Pavlovian story. Both need the aversive event to follow the content within seconds. [I], built on the Rescorla contingency idea [S] and the delay-of-punishment findings [S].
2. **The 10 s window is the biggest design flaw.** A decision made at window close means the GVS lands roughly 10 to 20 s after the behaviour started, plus processing [I, arithmetic]. The delayed-punishment literature says delays of 30 to 120 s already weaken suppression in humans, and the rat data say a longer delay needs a stronger shock for the same effect [S]. 10 to 20 s is inside the zone where it still works but worse. Move to short sliding windows so the stim lands within a few seconds [I].
3. **Nausea is a poor-belonging US for visual phone content.** Garcia and Koelling: in rats, illness conditioned to taste, not to audiovisual cues; shock did the reverse [S]. Quail are the known exception, because vision is their dominant food cue [S]. Humans get conditioned nausea to clinic context in chemotherapy [S], but those patients have strong nausea US and many pairings. GVS is a weak, short US. Expect weak conditioning from the Pavlovian route and rely on the operant route [I].
4. **Mild, brief GVS may not even cause nausea.** One safety study at 1.5 mA reported neither seizures, vertigo nor nausea; the common effects were itching, tingling and burning under the electrode [S]. So the "US" may be mostly skin sensation plus sway, not illness [I]. If it is not nauseating, the Garcia result is less relevant and it is just a mild punisher.
5. **Aversion technology has a poor record.** Cochrane: the rapid-smoking result was positive (OR 2.01) but unreliable; milder aversive methods showed nothing (OR 1.15); the one trial with biochemical validation was null [S]. Electric aversion therapy for sexual orientation was abandoned partly for ethics and partly because it did not work [S, commentary]. Pavlok has one registered feasibility trial and no results I could find [S].
6. **Recommended settings:** sliding 3 s windows every 1 s (decide when 2 consecutive windows agree), stim lands at most about 3 to 5 s after onset; duration 500 to 1000 ms at the lowest intensity that is clearly felt; continuous punishment first (every detected episode, subject to the ESP32 caps), not partial; 10 to 20 pairings per session, cap at the existing 30/hour; log reels minutes per day for 7 days baseline then 7 days on, with a sham-device control week if possible. Section 6 has the detail.

## 1. Classical conditioning or punishment?

**Classical (Pavlovian) reading.** The CS is the reels experience (visuals, audio, scrolling feel); the US is the GVS sensation; the UR is disorientation or nausea. After pairings, reels should evoke some of that unpleasantness. Rescorla argued that what matters is contingency, how well the CS predicts the US, not mere closeness in time; a CS that is paired with the US at the same rate as it is absent learns nothing [S: Rescorla 1967, https://about.illinoisstate.edu/vfdouga/files/2019/10//rescorla-pavlovian-conditioning-and-its-proper-control-procedures.pdf ; Rescorla 1988, https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych227/RESCORLA%20(1988).pdf]. Implication [I]: stimulating during reels and almost never otherwise is a good contingency; random stimulation unrelated to content would not condition.

**Operant (punishment) reading.** The stim is delivered because you did a behaviour (scrolling reels), and the aim is to reduce that behaviour. That is positive punishment. Lerman and Vorndran's review of the basic and applied punishment literature is the standard entry point [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284409]. It notes that research on conditioned, intermittent and delayed punishment is thin [S, via a commentary summary: https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/practitioner-development/punishment-happens--some-comments-on-lerman-and-vorndran-s-review/ ].

**Which predicts better?** [I] The operant frame, for three reasons.
- The device is response-contingent by construction, so the user's behaviour changes what happens next. Pavlovian conditioning does not require that; operant punishment does, and the system supplies it.
- GVS is a weak, short US. Pavlovian "dislike" of reels would need a strong, reliable UR. Punishment only needs the stim to be unpleasant enough to make the user stop or leave.
- Real demonstration outcome is "the user scrolls less", which is a behaviour measure, not an attitude measure.
The practical risk of the operant frame [S]: punishment suppresses the behaviour while it is in force and the behaviour recovers when it is removed (section 4).

**What each says about timing.** Both say the aversive event must be close to the behaviour or CS onset. Operant: seconds matter (section 2). Pavlovian: the CS should precede the US with the US arriving while the CS is still on (delay conditioning), not after it ends [S for the general delay-vs-trace distinction: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10199915/]. Taste aversion is the exception that allows hours, and only for taste (section 3).

## 2. Timing and latency

**Delay of punishment, sourced facts.**
- Humans: punishment delayed 30, 60 or 120 s suppressed an immediately rewarded response less than immediate punishment (Banks and Vogel-Sprott 1965, N = 40) [S: https://scilit.com/publications/17181ccbd76de0c3ca5ba1d9ac82a11d ; description via search summary].
- Rats: longer delay-of-shock intervals (0 to 28 s tested) shifted the shock intensity needed to suppress lever pressing upward (Cohen 1968) [S: https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/997224784 , citing the study design].
- Camp, Raymond and Church (1967), "Temporal relationship between response and punishment", J Exp Psychol 74:114-123, doi 10.1037/h0024518 is the classic study of this. I could not retrieve its numbers, so I make no claim about them.
- Textbook-level statement that immediate punishers work best, with no primary evidence in the pages I saw: https://txwes.pressbooks.pub/psychologyoflearningtxwes/chapter/07-3-factors-influencing-the-effectiveness-of-consequences

**Pavlovian interval, sourced.** In human delay eyeblink conditioning, CS-US intervals under about 250 ms give very few conditioned responses and about 400 to 500 ms is typically best [S: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10199915/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41539-023-00167-w ]. That is the eyeblink system, a motor reflex with a very short ideal interval. It does not transfer to slow nausea/disgust responses [I]. I found no human number for the optimal CS-US interval for conditioned disgust or nausea to an exteroceptive cue.

**What 10 s windows cost [I, arithmetic].**
- The classifier needs the whole window to measure flow shape. A reel that starts at second 1 of a window is not classified until second 10; one that starts at second 9 is classified at second 10 but with almost no evidence (and the 50 kB floor would block it).
- So time from behaviour onset to stim is roughly uniform between 0 and 10 s for windows that already contain the behaviour, plus the next window if the first lacked enough bytes, plus capture-to-decision processing (unmeasured), then the ESP32 stim of 0.5 to 2 s. Realistic: about 5 to 20 s, typical about 10 to 15 s.
- During those seconds the user has already swiped past several reels, so the stim attaches to whatever reel is on screen when it lands. That reel was not the one that triggered the decision. For a Pavlovian CS that is blurred; for operant punishment it is a delay on the order that the human data show is less effective but not zero.

**Window recommendation [I].** Keep the 10 s window only as a long-context confirmation. Add short sliding windows: 3 s width, computed every 1 s. Fire when two consecutive short windows classify the same bucket at or above threshold, which gives latency of about 4 s after reels traffic starts. Re-test the 50 kB floor, since it is calibrated for 10 s and will need scaling. The firmware cooldown (5 s) and hourly cap (30) already bound the damage of extra false positives. This needs a real capture to confirm the classifier still separates reels from DMs at 3 s; the repo README says it is not yet run on real traffic.

## 3. Nausea as the US (belongingness)

- **Garcia and Koelling 1966**, Psychonomic Science 4:123-124. Rats drinking flavoured water with a light and click: when sickness followed, they avoided the flavour but not the audiovisual cues; when shock followed, the reverse [S: https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03342209 ; summary https://www.americanscientist.org/node/3670 ]. A 1984 paper argued some of the deficit might be retrieval failure; its own reminder experiments did not support that, so the deficit looks like an acquisition failure [S, as relayed in the search summary of Schachtman, Kasprow and Miller 1984; I did not open that paper].
- **Long delays work for taste only.** Aversion to saccharin formed with illness delayed up to 75 min [S: search summary of Garcia's group; Kalat and Rozin 1973, https://psychology.hanover.edu/classes/Learning/papers/Kalat%20Rozin%201973.pdf ]. Revusky's account: long-delay learning needs no interfering events during the gap [S, same search]. A phone has constant interference, so this does not rescue a long delay for visual content [I].
- **Exception: quail.** Quail avoided water that was only darkened, even more than flavoured, after illness, since vision is their salient food cue [S: Wilcoxon, Dragoin and Kral 1971, Science 171:826-828, https://scilit.com/publications/97ac2b650277a44820493c1ebc84aae1 ]. The lesson is that belongingness tracks which cue the species uses to find food [I]; people are mostly taste/smell and vision foragers, with unclear mapping to a scrolling app.
- **Chemotherapy anticipatory nausea.** Patients develop nausea to clinic cues (sights, smells, nurses, room) with the drug as US; reviews: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880638 ; https://www.wjgnet.com/1007-9327/full/v13/i25/3430.htm ; https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00502/pdf . Reported up to 20% nausea before a cycle and up to 30% by cycle four in one review [S, via search summary]. This shows contextual and visual-ish cues can come to evoke nausea in humans [S], but the US is a strong systemic drug effect lasting hours, over repeated sessions. A GVS pulse of 0.5 to 2 s is far weaker [I]. The same reviews note that preventing the nausea (the US) is the best prevention of the conditioned version [S], which tells you US strength is the lever.
- **Rotation-chair conditioning in humans.** In the Klosterhalfen/Stockhorst paradigm, subjects drank a novel-tasting juice before being rotated; the conditioned group developed taste aversion after a single pairing, controls did not (n = 32) [S: Klosterhalfen et al. 2000, via https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/75827455 ]. Overshadowing and latent inhibition of rotation-induced nausea were also shown [S: https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/57887 ; https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~gh1/pdf/2005Klosterhalfen.pdf ]. The CS in these studies that I could confirm was taste; I did not find a human study that used a visual CS with a vestibular/rotation US. Treat that as unfound, not as absent. One later finding relayed in a summary: conditioning rules held for anticipatory nausea but not for post-rotation nausea [S, search summary: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4146778 ].
- **Visual-vestibular conflict is already a natural pairing.** Visually induced motion sickness comes from mismatch between visual and vestibular signals, and phones showing motion without body motion are a weak instance [S for the mismatch theory: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00472/pdf ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4403286 ]. [I] That is an argument that vestibular disruption can "belong" to moving visual content more than ordinary nausea belongs to light and click. It is a hypothesis, not a result I found tested for GVS.

**Verdict [I].** GVS nausea is a mediocre US for a visual CS by the Garcia criterion, helped by the visual-vestibular link and hurt by GVS being weak and brief. Better-belonging USes by the same logic:
- If the CS is audiovisual, the Garcia pattern says exteroceptive aversives (brief mild shock-like skin stimulation, a harsh sound) belong with audiovisual cues [S for the rat result above]. Pavlok-style wrist stim is that, not GVS.
- If you want a nausea/taste route, the CS would have to be gustatory, which a phone cannot supply.
- Keep GVS for the demo (it is the project's point) but treat it as a mild vestibular punisher and do not promise "nausea conditioning".

**Habituation of GVS itself.** Repeated GVS reduces its effect: sway responses fell across repeated sessions; a 12-week study of 10 subjects found impairment recovered to baseline after about 70 to 80 min of cumulative exposure, still present six months later [S: Dilda et al. 2014 PLoS ONE, https://doaj.org/article/b5b8f41492ee4aa58d5b3ae5fdb7eb24 ; Balter et al. 2004 via https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/habituation-to-galvanic-vestibular-stimulation/ ]. That is a postural effect in the lab, not nausea, and doses were longer than mine. Direction is clear though: expect the US to weaken over days [I]. I found no study of GVS used as a conditioning US.

## 4. Schedule, intensity, extinction, generalisation, discrimination

**Continuous vs partial.**
- Punishment: Azrin, Holz and Hake 1963 found fixed-ratio (intermittent) punishment produced less rapid and less complete suppression than continuous punishment [S: abstract via https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1404287 ]. A later review cited by the same search qualifies it as possibly true only when responding is still rewarded; scrolling is rewarded, so it applies [S for the qualification; I for the application].
- Pavlovian partial reinforcement: evidence in humans is mixed on whether it slows extinction; one fear-conditioning review found limited evidence it extends extinction while it weakens acquisition [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4749731 ; https://mdpi.com/2076-328X/14/8/630 , via search summaries]. [I] Start continuous for acquisition; there is no good reason to go partial early. The 30/hour and 5 s cooldown make the schedule partial anyway whenever scrolling is heavy.

**Intensity.** Punishment effectiveness depends on intensity, and delay and intensity trade off (stronger needed if later) [S: Cohen 1968, link above]. Cochrane found a borderline dose-response for aversive smoking (OR 1.67, 95% CI 0.99 to 2.81) with the authors calling the evidence insufficient [S: https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000546_does-smoking-way-unpleasant-help-smokers-quit]. GVS safety data: itching, tingling, burning at the electrode are the common effects; 1.5 mA caused few and mild adverse events with no nausea or vertigo observed; headache about 12% and dizziness 3% in a larger trial of up to 0.5 mA for an hour [S: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21879800 ; https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.3109/02699052.2011.607789 ; parameter review https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11841469/ ]. Perception threshold is around 0.75 to 1 mA in one analysis [S, same search]. [I] Keep per-bucket weight scaling but set the floor at a clearly perceptible level, because below threshold there is nothing to learn from.

**Extinction and relapse.** After punishment is removed, performance recovered almost completely after a compensatory burst (Holz, Azrin and Ulrich 1963), and a follow-up reported rates eventually returned to pre-punishment levels [S: search summary of https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1333721 ; I only saw the summary]. Pavlovian extinction is new learning that is specific to context, so responding can return when the CS is met in a different context (renewal) [S: Bouton 2004, Learn Mem 11:485-494, https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/56147/20331_ftp.pdf;sequence=1 ]. [I] Once the device comes off (new context: no wristband/electrode), expect the CS to lose its force fast. A demo effect that lasts only while the device is worn is the honest expected outcome.

**Generalisation.** In differential conditioning the highest response goes to the CS+ and falls for stimuli less like it; the steepness of the gradient measures how much generalises [S: Lissek et al. 2008, https://angst.dl8.umn.edu/sites/angst.umn.edu/files/files/lissek20082.pdf ]. Partial-reinforcement groups showed more generalisation in one study [S: MDPI link above]. [I] Reels and DMs share an app, domain and UI, so generalisation from reels to "Instagram" is likely, and to "the phone" is plausible. Discrimination training is what is meant to stop that.

**Discrimination (reels punished, DMs not).**
- Pavlovian: standard differential conditioning has a CS+ and an unpaired CS-; a human example used 10 CS+ and 10 CS- trials in acquisition [S: Dunsmoor and LaBar 2013, https://sites.utexas.edu/dunsmoorlab/wp-content/uploads/sites/4333/2017/07/2013-BNS-Dunsmoor-LaBar.pdf ]. That is one lab's protocol, not a required minimum. I found no source giving a minimum trial count for discrimination.
- Operant: punishment can come under stimulus control, but the literature on it is described as small, and it is unclear whether the cue or the punisher delivery does the controlling [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1404125 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10358787 ; Broomer and Bouton 2023 found punishment, like extinction, is response-specific]. One pigeon study found a stimulus present during contingent shock did not itself become punishing, unlike under non-contingent pairing [S: summary from the same search].
- [I] What this means here: the unpunished DM activity is the CS- and needs to be reliably free of stims. Any false positive in DMs (the classifier shares cdninstagram.com between them, README) both reduces discrimination and teaches the wrong thing. Treat DM false positives as costlier than reels misses; the existing "winner must beat runner-up at 0.90" rule and weight 0 for DMs are in the right direction.

## 5. Real aversion therapy and wearable punishment

- **Pavlok.** One registered randomised trial (UAB, N = 48, device on vs off, smoking, primary aim acceptability and feasibility; listed completed) with no results found [S: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03450148 ]. Company success claims (over 50%, 75% cut or quit) are anecdote, not controlled data [S, news items: https://www.irishtimes.com/business/pavlok-zapping-bad-habits-with-450-volts-1.2498378 ; critique blog https://clinicalpsychreading.blogspot.com/2016/05/aversive-shock-therapy-for-bad-habits.html ]. I found no peer-reviewed efficacy result.
- **Smoking.** Cochrane (Hajek and Stead), 25 trials, 12 with rapid smoking: rapid smoking OR 2.01 (1.36 to 2.95); other aversion methods OR 1.15 (0.73 to 1.82); most trials had serious methodological problems likely to give spurious positives; asymmetric funnel plot; the one trial with full biochemical validation was non-significant; conclusion: insufficient evidence [S: https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000546_does-smoking-way-unpleasant-help-smokers-quit ; https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000546.pub2/pdf/CDSR/CD000546/CD000546_abstract.pdf ; full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7045729 ].
- **Alcohol (emetic).** Uncontrolled follow-ups report high abstinence: Voegtlin 1950, 4,468 patients, about 44% continuously abstinent among those followed; Smith and Frawley 1990, 71.3% at 12 months; Elkins et al. 1991 appraisal: private hospitals around 60% at one year, with few experimental evaluations [S: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1741730/ ; https://filtermag.org/aversion-therapy-alcohol/ ]. These are selected, self-paying, multimodal-programme populations; no controlled effect size found. Schick Shadel Hospital in Burien closed 30 June 2022 [S: filtermag link].
- **Electric aversion for sexual orientation.** MacCulloch and Feldman 1967 (BMJ) reported about 60% improvement and inspired many clinics; commentary says it was largely abandoned for ethics and because it did not work [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC344258 ; https://www.bmj.com/content/328/7437/429.full.pdf ; https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/documents/news/conversion-therapy-full-document-final-8-june-2022.pdf ; the "didn't work" line is from a blog commentary https://www.boxturtlebulletin.com/2016/06/03/72841 so weakest]. Patient account: waiting for the shock was worse than the shock [S: https://bps.org.uk/psychologist/aversion-therapy-personal-account ]. This is an ethical warning about consent and purpose, not a comparison to a self-chosen focus aid [I].
- **Why it fell out of clinical use [S/I mix].** Weak or unvalidated outcomes, relapse once the aversive setting goes (section 4), and ethics. Clinic-based emetic therapy persists in niche settings.

## 6. Recommendations (each tied to a source)

1. **Cut decision latency to about 4 s with 3 s windows every 1 s, requiring two consecutive hits.** Reason: delayed punishment suppresses less and needs more intensity (Banks and Vogel-Sprott; Cohen, section 2); a 10 s window adds 5 to 20 s [I]. Validate on real traces first.
2. **Do not let the stim land on the next reel.** Log the video/flow start time and the stim time, and report the median onset-to-stim latency in the demo. Tied to section 2.
3. **Stim duration 500 to 1000 ms at the lowest level that is clearly felt; use the 2000 ms only for testing.** Tied to GVS safety data (mild effects, mostly skin sensation) and the weak-US concern (section 3). Do not raise current to chase nausea: GVS safety sources describe mild effects at 1.5 mA, and nothing I found supports pushing further.
4. **Continuous schedule at the start: stim every qualifying episode, subject to the 5 s cooldown and 30/hour caps.** Tied to Azrin, Holz and Hake 1963 (intermittent suppresses less) and the mixed partial-reinforcement evidence.
5. **Pairings: about 10 to 20 per session, capped well under 30/hour; one or two sessions a day for a week.** There is no sourced number for a minimum. The only anchor I have is the 10 + 10 trials in a human differential-conditioning protocol (Dunsmoor and LaBar) and single-pairing taste results in rotation studies [both S]. The rest is [I]; state it as a guess.
6. **Treat DMs as the CS-.** Keep weight 0, require all DM traffic to produce zero stims in the log, and review any DM false positive manually. Tied to discrimination literature (section 4).
7. **Expect habituation and plan a rotation of intensity within the safe range, not an escalation.** Tied to Dilda et al. 2014 and Balter et al. 2004 (GVS effects decline with repeated exposure).
8. **Measure behaviour from logs, not memory.** Primary: minutes of reels per day from the pipeline's own flow log (or iOS Screen Time), 7 days baseline with the device attached but inert, 7 days active, 7 days device off. Self-report as a secondary measure only, because self-reported screen time rarely matches logged use [S: Parry et al. 2021, Nat Hum Behav, https://www.core.ac.uk/download/481136497.pdf ; https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/05/210517124919.htm ]. Add a 3-item daily rating (urge to open reels 0 to 10, how unpleasant reels feel 0 to 10, DMs unaffected yes/no).
9. **Include a sham week (stim off, same hardware) before or after the active week.** It separates "awareness of being monitored" from the stim; Rescorla's control logic requires a zero-contingency comparison [S: Rescorla 1967]. Pavlok critics attribute apparent benefits to reminder effects [S: blog above], which is exactly what a sham week tests.
10. **Test for extinction and context.** Remove the device and keep logging for a week; expect return to baseline (Bouton 2004; Holz et al. 1963). Report that number.
11. **Demo framing, honest version [I].** "This is a self-run experiment in positive punishment of a habit, with a Pavlovian side effect I do not expect to be strong. GVS gives a brief vestibular and skin sensation; nausea conditioning to visual content is not supported by the classic literature. Evidence for aversive wearables is weak (Cochrane, Pavlok trial with no results); results will be N = 1, one week, and I will show the logs including the sham week and what happened after removal." Do not claim cure, do not claim nausea conditioning, and do not show a single before/after as proof.
12. **Safety and consent.** User controls their own arming switch; the caps stay in firmware; stop on headache, persistent dizziness or skin irritation; no use on anyone else, nobody with implants, epilepsy or pregnancy without medical advice. GVS adverse effects (headache, dizziness, skin burning) are documented [S, section 4 links]. The history of electric aversion therapy is a reason to keep the consent and purpose self-chosen and clear [I].

## 7. Open questions

- Does the 3 s window still separate reels from DMs on real QUIC traffic? The README says only synthetic traffic has been tested.
- What is the real GVS onset-to-sensation latency, and does a 0.5 to 2 s pulse at the intended current produce any nausea, or only tingling and sway? I found no data for conditioning-relevant doses; this needs a self-test.
- Is there a human study with a visual CS and a vestibular/rotation US? My searches did not find one; absence in search is not absence in the literature (Klosterhalfen/Stockhorst group publication lists are the place to check).
- Minimum trial count for discriminative aversive conditioning in humans: not found.
- Camp, Raymond and Church 1967 numbers (the standard human-adjacent delay-of-punishment study): not retrieved; get the abstract before quoting any seconds.
- Lerman and Vorndran 2002 on delay and intermittency: I could only read summaries, not the text.
- Does it generalise from reels to all of Instagram, or to the phone? No direct evidence; log time in other apps during the active week to see substitution (people may just move to another feed).
